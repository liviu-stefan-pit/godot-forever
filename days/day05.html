Concepts you need first

Tiles in Godot 4: five words

  • TileSet (a Resource): the catalogue. It owns the tile size, one or more sources (atlas = a sprite sheet cut into tiles), and the layers of per-tile data: physics, occlusion, navigation, terrains, custom data.
  • TileMapLayer (a Node2D): one grid of cells that uses a TileSet. A cell holds (source id, atlas coordinates, alternative id). You stack several TileMapLayer nodes to get background, ground and hazards. The old TileMap node is deprecated; this course never uses it.
  • Terrain: autotiling. Tiles carry "peering bits" that say which neighbours they connect to, so painting a blob of ground automatically picks edge and corner tiles.
  • Physics layer (inside the TileSet): lets a tile carry collision polygons. It has its own collision layer/mask bits, like any physics body.
  • Custom data layer: your own typed field per tile. Today: damage (int) on the spike tile.
Coming from Unity / Unreal?

TileSet is like a Unity Tile Palette plus Rule Tiles; a TileMapLayer is a Unity Tilemap; terrains replace Rule Tiles. In Unreal terms it is Paper2D's TileSet + TileMap. The big Godot difference: collision shapes live inside the TileSet and are merged into physics bodies for you.

A jump you can reason about

Tuning gravity and jump speed by eye is slow. Instead choose two numbers that mean something to a designer: jump_height (pixels) and time_to_apex (seconds). Constant-acceleration physics then gives both constants:

gravity        = 2 * jump_height / time_to_apex^2     (px/s^2)
jump_velocity  = -2 * jump_height / time_to_apex      (px/s, negative = up in 2D)

jump_height = 112 px (3.5 tiles), time_to_apex = 0.4 s
=> gravity = 1400 px/s^2, jump_velocity = -560 px/s

Then three feel tricks every good platformer uses:

  • Heavier fall: multiply gravity while falling (we use 1.6x). Rising feels floaty-light, falling feels snappy.
  • Variable jump height: when the player releases the jump button while still rising, cut the upward velocity (multiply by 0.4). A tap is a hop, a hold is a full jump.
  • Forgiveness: coyote time (can still jump for 0.1 s after walking off a ledge) and jump buffering (a press up to 0.12 s before landing is remembered and fires on landing). Players never notice these; they only notice their absence.

move_and_slide() in one minute

A CharacterBody2D is moved by you, not by the physics engine. You set velocity (pixels per second) and call move_and_slide() once per physics frame. The engine moves the body, slides along walls and floors, then modifies velocity (hitting the floor zeroes the vertical part) and updates is_on_floor(), is_on_wall(), is_on_ceiling(). Consequences:

  • is_on_floor() describes the previous move_and_slide(); read it before moving for "was I grounded?" logic.
  • velocity is already in px/s, so move_and_slide() multiplies by delta internally. Never multiply the velocity by delta yourself. Only the changes (gravity * delta) get delta.
  • Everything goes into _physics_process, not _process.

Terrain modes in one glance

Connect joins every painted cell with its neighbours on the same layer (what you used). Path only connects cells within the same mouse stroke: useful for two roads that touch without merging. Match Corners and Sides, Match Corners and Match Sides are the three terrain set modes (what peering bits exist). Corners+Sides needs up to 47 tiles for a seamless set; we chose Match Sides so our 16-tile sheet is complete.

Walk through the controller once

Order inside _physics_process matters: timers (what is the ground state?), gravity, jump decision, horizontal, then one single move_and_slide(). Reading is_on_floor() before the move gives last frame's truth, which is exactly what coyote time wants. Gravity is skipped while grounded so velocity.y stays 0 and the body does not "vibrate" against the floor. move_toward(a, b, step) moves a toward b by at most step per call, so acceleration and friction are just different step sizes.

Why a rectangle and no slopes?

Rectangular bodies on rectangular tiles are the most predictable platformer setup: no snagging, exact jump arcs. With slopes you would add floor_snap_length and tune floor_max_angle. See CharacterBody2D in the class reference when you need them.

Version note

This day needs Godot 4.3+ (TileMapLayer exists since 4.3). The Inspector label for the TileMap editor's bottom panel is still "TileMap". If your 4.x release shows a slightly different dropdown name for Paint Properties, use the Select-mode alternative given in the collision step.

Common pitfalls

  • Setting the Tile Size after adding the atlas. Auto-creation uses the size at that moment. Fix: set 32x32, then in the TileSet panel run Create Tiles in Non-Transparent Texture Regions.
  • Editing a built-in TileSet in one layer and expecting the others to follow. Save the TileSet to .tres first; duplicated layers then share it. Tile changes made before saving only live in that layer.
  • Forgetting to press Ctrl+S after editing the external TileSet. The resource is only written to disk with the scene save.
  • Multiplying velocity by delta. move_and_slide() does it. If your character crawls at 5 px/s, you did it twice.
  • Reading is_on_floor() after changing velocity but before move_and_slide(). It will tell you about last frame. Design with that in mind (coyote time relies on it).
  • Player mask without world. The player drops through everything. Check Layer vs Mask names by hovering.
  • Pressing F6 on the wrong tab. F6 runs the scene in the active tab. The player scene alone has nothing to stand on.
  • Mixing up time_to_apex and total air time. Apex is only the way up (0.4 s); with the 1.6x fall multiplier the way down is faster (about 0.3 s).

Challenge (optional, 20-30 min)

Apex hang and faster turns

Two classic polish items. (1) Apex modifier: when absf(velocity.y) is below, say, 60 px/s while the jump button is held, halve gravity for a moment: a short "hang" at the top that makes precise landings easier. (2) Snappy turns: when the input direction is opposite to the current velocity.x, apply ground_friction + ground_acceleration as the step so you reverse in a few frames instead of sliding.

Hint

Both live in existing functions: the first in _apply_gravity (check Input.is_action_pressed("jump")), the second in _handle_horizontal (compare signf(input_x) with signf(velocity.x)). Expose the thresholds as @export variables and tune them live in the Remote tree.

Check yourself

You multiply velocity by delta before calling move_and_slide(). What happens?

  • Nothing, it is required in Godot 4
  • The character moves far too slowly, because move_and_slide() already applies delta
  • The character moves twice as fast
  • A runtime error

velocity is in px/s and move_and_slide() scales it by the physics step internally. Only accelerations (gravity, friction) are multiplied by delta when you change the velocity.

Why does the controller derive gravity and jump speed from jump_height and time_to_apex?

  • It is faster to compute at runtime
  • Godot requires it
  • Designers can reason about the jump (height, time) and the two constants always stay consistent
  • It removes the need for move_and_slide()

With two meaningful numbers, g = 2h / t^2 and v = -2h / t give a parabola that peaks at exactly that height and time.

The terrain set mode is Match Sides. How many neighbours does each tile's peering bits describe?

  • 4 (top, right, bottom, left)
  • 8 (sides and corners)
  • 4 corners only
  • 1 (the tile below)

Match Sides checks the four edge neighbours; Match Corners checks the four diagonals; Match Corners and Sides checks all eight (up to 47 tiles for a complete set).

A player presses jump 80 ms before touching the ground and the jump still happens on landing. Which technique is that?

  • Coyote time
  • Variable jump height
  • A bug
  • Jump buffering

Jump buffering stores the press for a short window (0.12 s here). Coyote time is the opposite: jumping shortly after leaving a ledge.

Which node holds the grid of painted cells, and which resource holds tile shapes, terrains and custom data?

  • TileMap and TileSet
  • TileMapLayer and TileSet
  • TileSet and TileMapLayer
  • Sprite2D and AtlasTexture

The TileMapLayer node stores cells; the TileSet resource defines tiles. The old TileMap node is deprecated.
