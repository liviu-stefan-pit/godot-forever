Concepts you need first

The 3D coordinate system

Godot uses a right-handed, Y-up coordinate system, and 1 unit equals 1 meter. Physics and lighting are tuned for that scale, so a human-sized character is roughly 1.7 units tall and a jump of 1.5 units is a normal jump. The axes are always colored the same way: X red (sideways, +X is right), Y green (up), Z blue (front/back).

The one rule to memorize: forward is -Z. A Camera3D, a light and anything you call look_at() on points along its own -Z. In code, Vector3.FORWARD is (0, 0, -1), Vector3.UP is (0, 1, 0), and the ground plane is XZ (not XY as in 2D).

Coming from Unity or Unreal?

Unity is left-handed with +Z forward; Unreal is Z-up. Godot's -Z forward matches Blender's glTF export convention and OpenGL. If your movement code from another engine "goes backward", negate Z. Imported glTF models already follow Godot's convention.

2D to 3D mapping

You know (2D)3D equivalentWhat changes
Node2DNode3Dposition, rotation and scale become Vector3s. Rotation is three Euler angles, not one float.
CharacterBody2DCharacterBody3DSame velocity + move_and_slide(). Gravity pulls along -Y; you move on X and Z.
Area2D, CollisionShape2DArea3D, CollisionShape3DShapes are volumes (box, sphere, capsule...). Same layer and mask bits.
Camera2DCamera3DA scene with no camera renders nothing. Perspective by default, with a fov.
Sprite2DMeshInstance3DA mesh plus a material. Lights and shadows are real, so you need a light.

Node3D transforms and the Basis

Every Node3D has a Transform3D: an origin (position) and a basis (a 3x3 matrix holding rotation and scale). The three columns of the basis are the node's local X, Y and Z axes expressed in the parent's space. That is why -transform.basis.z is "where this node is facing", and why some_basis * Vector3(1, 0, 0) means "rotate this vector into that orientation". We use exactly that trick today: build a basis from the camera's yaw and multiply the stick input by it to get camera-relative movement.

The Inspector shows Position / Rotation / Scale because that is easier to read. In code those are position, rotation (radians) and rotation_degrees, and scale. Plus the global_ variants (global_position, global_basis, global_transform) which are relative to the world rather than the parent.

Rotation caveats that bite everyone once
  • Euler angles are order-dependent. rotation applies Y, then X, then Z (order YXZ). Setting yaw on one node and pitch on a child node (what our camera does) avoids gimbal lock entirely, because each node only rotates around one axis.
  • Never lerp() an angle. Blending 350° to 10° with lerp spins the long way around. Use lerp_angle(from, to, weight) for a single angle (shortest path), and a Quaternion or Basis.slerp() for full orientations.
  • Do not scale nodes you move or collide with. Scale the visual child instead, and change shape sizes for collision. Non-uniform scale on a physics body leads to warnings and strange collisions.
  • Precision drift. Repeatedly multiplying transforms accumulates floating-point error; if you do that, call orthonormalized() now and then. Setting angles directly (what we do) never drifts.
  • Rotating the body vs rotating a pivot. Rotating the physics body also rotates the camera if the camera is its child. We rotate a child Pivot (and the visible model inside it) so the camera and collision capsule keep their orientation.

Read the official Using 3D transforms page later; it is the best explanation of why Godot prefers transforms to Euler angles.

Today's building blocks in one paragraph each

  • CSG (Constructive Solid Geometry). Nodes like CSGBox3D and CSGCylinder3D that you combine with boolean operations (union, subtraction, intersection) inside a CSGCombiner3D. They are for prototyping: fast to build and walkable with Use Collision, slower than baked meshes at runtime. Perfect for an arena.
  • glTF / .glb. Godot's preferred 3D format. When a .glb lands in res://, Godot imports it as a scene you can drag into your own scenes. You cannot edit the file itself (Godot never overwrites sources); you wrap it in your own scene and tweak there.
  • CharacterBody3D + SpringArm3D. The body moves with velocity and move_and_slide(). A SpringArm3D sweeps a shape backward from a pivot and slides its child camera forward when something is in the way, so the camera never clips through walls.
  • Environment. A WorldEnvironment node holds an Environment resource: sky, ambient light, tonemapping, glow, ambient occlusion and fog. 3D without it looks flat.

  1. Start the main scene. In the menu bar choose Scene > New Scene. In the Scene dock (top-left) click 3D Scene. A Node3D root appears and the editor jumps to the 3D workspace: a grid with red (X) and blue (Z) lines and a green (Y) line you see when looking from the side. Press F2, rename the root Main, press Ctrl+S and save it as res://game/main.tscn.
  2. Add the arena root. Select Main, press Ctrl+A, type CSGCombiner3D, select it and click Create. Rename it Arena. A CSGCombiner3D is an empty "container" whose children are merged with boolean operations, in tree order. Everything we build today is a child of Arena, and the Arena node is the single "CSG root" that will carry the collision.
  3. Add the arena floor and wall block (Base). Select Arena, press Ctrl+A, search CSGCylinder3D, Create, rename it Base. In the Inspector (right side) set Radius 21, Height 6, Sides 48 (more sides = rounder). Expand Transform (it is a section lower in the Inspector, under Node3D) and set Position to (0, 2, 0). A big grey drum appears: it spans from y = -1 to y = 5, so its top is not the floor yet.
  4. Practice orbit, zoom and focus. With Base selected, hover the viewport and press F to frame it. Orbit with the middle mouse button to look from above, then from the side, then from underneath. Click the green circle on the view gizmo for a side view and the blue circle for a front view. Press keypad 5 to flip between perspective and orthogonal projection, then back. Orthogonal is the best view for aligning CSG shapes.
  5. Hollow it out with a subtraction. Select Arena, add another CSGCylinder3D, rename it Hollow. Set Radius 20, Height 6, Sides 48, Position (0, 3, 0). Then, under the CSGShape3D section of the Inspector, set Operation to Subtraction (script name operation). The drum becomes a bowl: a 1 m thick floor (top at y = 0) and a wall ring from radius 20 to 21. The result is the first shape minus the second, so Hollow must stay below Base in the Scene dock.
  6. Add the four pillars. Select Arena, add a CSGCylinder3D named Pillar1 with Radius 1.2, Height 5.2, Sides 16, Position (8, 2.5, 8), Operation left on Union. Select Pillar1 and press Ctrl+D (duplicate) three times; rename the copies Pillar2, Pillar3, Pillar4 and set their positions to (-8, 2.5, 8), (8, 2.5, -8) and (-8, 2.5, -8). Pillars start 0.1 m below the floor on purpose so they fuse with it and leave no seam.
  7. Add the dais. Select Arena, add a CSGBox3D named Dais: Size (10, 1, 6), Position (0, 0.4, -10). Its top sits at y = 0.9, a raised platform at the north side of the arena.
  8. Add the ramp and learn the rotate gizmo. Add a CSGBox3D named Ramp: Size (4, 0.4, 6.2), Position (0, 0.22, -4). Now rotate it with the gizmo, as practice: press E (rotate mode), hover the viewport, drag the red arc a little, then hold Ctrl while dragging to feel the snapping (the angle appears in the bottom-left of the viewport). Press Ctrl+Z until the rotation is back at 0, then type the exact value: in the Inspector set Transform > Rotation to (9, 0, 0). The far end lifts to meet the dais (9° over 6 m is a 0.9 m rise), and the near end sinks slightly below the floor, which is hidden.
  9. Add two cover blocks. Add a CSGBox3D named Block1: Size (2.5, 1.2, 2.5), Position (-10, 0.5, 4), Rotation (0, 30, 0). Duplicate it (Ctrl+D), name the copy Block2 and set Position (11, 0.5, 2), Rotation (0, -20, 0). Try the T key (Use Local Space) with the move gizmo on Block1: with it on, the arrows follow the block's rotated axes; off, they are the world axes. Leave it off afterwards.
  10. Make it solid. Select the Arena node (the CSGCombiner3D root) and tick Use Collision in the Inspector (script name use_collision). This only works on the root of a CSG tree; it generates a static collision shape for the whole merged geometry. Collision layer stays on 1 (world) and the mask on 1. Never tick it on the children.

Optional: a stone texture without any asset (triplanar)

CSG nodes have no UV maps, so textures normally smear. Triplanar mapping projects the texture from three axes and fixes that. Try it on mat_stone.tres: expand Albedo, click the empty Texture field and choose New NoiseTexture2D; click it, set Noise to New FastNoiseLite and enable Seamless. Then expand UV1, tick Triplanar and set Scale to (0.15, 0.15, 0.15). You get a subtle mottled stone look. Triplanar is a bit slower than normal mapping, so it is fine for a prototype arena but would be baked or replaced in a shipping game. (Kenney offers CC0 prototype textures if you want grids: kenney.nl/assets, search "prototype textures"; verify on the page.)

The editor draws a preview sun and a preview sky whenever a scene has no real light or environment. Those previews exist only in the editor. A running game with neither a DirectionalLight3D nor a WorldEnvironment is a flat grey void. You are about to replace both previews with nodes you own.

SSAO and the renderer

Block 5: The player and the third-person camera

Block 2 already imported art/player.glb and art/mob.glb, named the seven 3D physics layers, and created the Input Map (move_left, move_right, move_up, move_down, jump, dash, attack, heavy_attack, look_left, look_right, look_up, look_down, pause). This block only consumes that setup. dash, attack, and heavy_attack stay quiet until Day 11. If any of those actions or either .glb is missing, go back to Block 2 before continuing.

The official Squash the Creeps lesson wraps player.glb in a sphere of about 0.8 m radius. We use a capsule instead, so the brawler does not roll, and we orbit a SpringArm3D instead of a fixed camera. A SpringArm3D casts along its own Z axis (Godot's "back" is +Z, and a camera looks down -Z, so an unrotated arm sits the camera behind a character that faces -Z) and pulls its children in when the cast hits. Leave its Shape empty: a direct child Camera3D then uses the camera's near-plane pyramid, which is the setup the spring-arm tutorial recommends.

Build the player scene

  1. Start a new scene. Scene > New Scene. In the Scene dock click Other Node, search CharacterBody3D, select it, and click Create. Press F2, rename it Player. Press Ctrl+S and save as res://player/player.tscn.
  2. Add the capsule. Select Player, press Ctrl+A, add a CollisionShape3D. In the Inspector click the empty Shape field and choose New CapsuleShape3D. Click the shape to expand it. Set Radius to 0.55 and Height to 1.6 (height includes the two hemispheres, and it must be at least twice the radius). Set the CollisionShape3D node's Position to (0, 0.8, 0) so the bottom of the capsule sits on local y = 0, which will be the floor.
  3. Collision layers. Select Player. In the Inspector, under Collision, set Layer to only player (layer 2: turn layer 1 off). Set Mask to world and enemies (layers 1 and 3). The body walks on the arena and bumps into enemies. It does not scan its own attack layer. Under the same node, enable Floor Constant Speed and set Floor Snap Length to 0.4 so the 9° ramp does not slow you or launch you off the lip. The script sets those two again, so a missed click is not fatal, but the Inspector should still match.
  4. Put the player in the group player. With Player selected, click the Node tab (the tab next to Inspector). Open the Groups sub-tab. In the field, type player and click Add. Day 11 finds the player with this group.
  5. Add Pivot and Rig. Select Player, add a Node3D, rename it Pivot. Select Pivot, add another Node3D, rename it Rig. Pivot is what the script yaws toward the movement direction, and what Day 11 keyframes for attacks. Rig is only for fitting the imported mesh. Never scale Player itself.
  6. Instance the model. From the FileSystem dock, drag art/player.glb onto Rig in the Scene dock. A child appears (its name comes from the file). Press F2 and rename that instance Model. Hover the viewport and press F with Model selected. The official model is a small rounded creature, roughly the size of the capsule. If the feet float or sink, move Rig's Position Y until the feet meet the bottom of the capsule. If the mesh is much larger or smaller than the capsule, change only Rig's Scale (start from (1, 1, 1) and nudge). If, once you can walk, the face travels backward, set Rig's Rotation Y to 180.
  7. Build the camera rig. Select Player and add a Node3D named CameraYaw. Set its Position to (0, 1.0, 0) (about chest height). Select CameraYaw and add a Node3D named CameraPitch. Leave its transform at identity. Yaw lives only on CameraYaw, pitch only on CameraPitch, which is the gimbal-lock avoidance from the concepts section. Select CameraPitch and add a SpringArm3D. Set Spring Length to 4 and Margin to 0.2. Leave Shape empty. Under Collision, set the spring arm's Mask to only world (layer 1), so enemies walking past the lens do not yank the camera in.
  8. Add the camera. Select SpringArm3D and add a Camera3D as its direct child (the pyramid cast only works for a direct child). Turn on Current so this camera is the one the game uses. Set Fov to 70. Leave the camera's position at (0, 0, 0): the spring arm writes that position every physics frame.

Why the arm excludes the player

The capsule is a physics body on a layer the arm might still see if the mask is wrong, and even a mask of world is not the whole story: the cast starts at the arm origin, inside the character. add_excluded_object(get_rid()) in _ready removes the player's own body from the cast. Without it the camera locks inside the mesh.

The controller script

  1. Attach the script. Right-click Player in the Scene dock and choose Attach Script. Set Path to res://player/player.gd, leave the template empty if you are offered one, and click Create. The Script workspace opens. Press Ctrl+A and replace the whole file with the listing, then Ctrl+S.

    get_vector("move_left", "move_right", "move_up", "move_down") puts W at y = -1. Vector3(x, 0, y) is then (0, 0, -1), which is Vector3.FORWARD. Multiplying by a basis built from the camera yaw turns that into "forward on the XZ plane from the camera". lerp_angle turns Pivot the short way. screen_relative is the unscaled mouse delta, so look speed does not change when the window size changes. Positive pitch looks up, and the clamp is tighter upward (+35°) than downward (-50°) because looking up swings the camera down toward the floor. Jump speed 6 against gravity 9.8 is about a 1.8 m hop. Walk speed is 6 m/s.

  2. Drop the player into the arena. Open res://game/main.tscn (click the scene tab, or double-click it in FileSystem; if Godot asks to save player.tscn, save it). Drag player/player.tscn from FileSystem onto Main. Select the new Player instance and set Position to (0, 0.2, 6), just above the floor, south of the center, facing the dais.
  3. Make main.tscn the main scene. Press F5. If a dialog asks you to pick the main scene, choose res://game/main.tscn and confirm. (The same slot is Project > Project Settings > Application > Run > Main Scene.) The game window opens, the mouse cursor disappears, and you are looking at the arena from behind the creature.

In the game window: W A S D move relative to where the camera looks, not relative to the world axes. Moving the mouse orbits; you cannot flip under the floor or straight over the top. Space jumps and you land on the floor, the ramp, and the dais. Walk at a pillar: the camera slides forward instead of entering the stone. The model turns to face the move direction. Press Esc and the cursor comes back (the game does not pause yet). Click the game window to capture again. Press F8 to stop. If you fall forever, Arena's Use Collision is off or the player's mask does not include world. If the camera is inside the mesh, SpringArm3D is not a child of CameraPitch, or its rotation is not (0, 0, 0). If the Output panel says an InputMap action does not exist, that action from Block 2 was never created. If F6 on player.tscn alone falls forever, that is expected: the player scene has no floor. Test with F5.

Common pitfalls

  • Captured mouse, and you cannot click Stop. F8 still stops the project from the editor. Esc in today's build only shows the cursor; it does not pause physics. Day 11's HUD takes over pause.
  • Blank game window. Camera3D is missing, is not a child of the spring arm, or Current is off. The editor viewport can look fine while the running game has no current camera.
  • Camera stuck in the character's head. The spring arm is casting against the capsule. Confirm _ready calls add_excluded_object(get_rid()) and the arm's mask is only layer 1.
  • Movement is backward when the camera looks north. move_up must be the negative Y argument of get_vector, which the script already does. If you edited the call and swapped the last two action names, forward and back flip.
  • The body slides down the ramp or launches off the lip. Floor Snap Length is still the default 0.1, or Floor Constant Speed is off. The script sets both; if you deleted those lines, put them back.
  • You scaled Player to fit the mesh. Scale Rig only. A scaled physics body warns and collides wrong.
  • Shadows missing in game, present in the editor. You were looking at the preview sun. The preview is editor-only. Sun needs Shadow > Enabled.
  • move_and_slide with an argument. Godot 4 reads the velocity property. Passing a vector is a Godot 3 call and will not parse.

Challenge (optional, about 25 min)

Mouse-wheel camera distance

While the mouse is captured, the wheel should change SpringArm3D.spring_length, clamped between 2.0 and 6.0, in steps of 0.4. Wheel up moves closer.

Hints

Handle it in _unhandled_input. The event type is InputEventMouseButton. Wheel ticks use MOUSE_BUTTON_WHEEL_UP and MOUSE_BUTTON_WHEEL_DOWN, and button.pressed is true for a tick. Write the result back to _spring_arm.spring_length. Do not put the camera on a second node between the arm and the Camera3D: the arm only uses the camera pyramid when the camera is a direct child.

Check yourself

Which way does a Godot Camera3D look, in its own local axes?

  • Along +Z.
  • Along -Z. Vector3.FORWARD is (0, 0, -1).
  • Along +Y.
  • Along +X.

Godot is Y-up and -Z forward. An unrotated SpringArm3D casts along +Z, which places the camera behind a character that faces -Z.

What does SpringArm3D actually do with its children?

  • It copies the parent's velocity onto the camera.
  • It casts along its Z axis and moves its direct children to the hit, shortened by margin.
  • It is a Camera3D with a smoothing property.
  • It bakes a navigation mesh behind the player.

Length is spring_length. With Shape empty and a direct child camera, the cast uses the camera's near-plane pyramid. Exclude the player's RID or the cast hits the capsule immediately.

Why does the look code read InputEventMouseMotion.screen_relative instead of relative?

  • relative is scaled by the stretch factor, so sensitivity would change with the window size. screen_relative is the raw screen delta.
  • screen_relative is in radians already.
  • relative only exists for gamepads.
  • They are the same value under a different name.

screen_relative has been on the event since Godot 4.3, which is why the version note at the top calls it out. Captured mode also needs Input.mouse_mode = Input.MOUSE_MODE_CAPTURED, or the cursor never locks.

How do you move a CharacterBody3D in Godot 4?

  • Pass the velocity vector into move_and_slide.
  • Write the velocity property (meters per second, not multiplied by delta), then call move_and_slide() with no arguments.
  • Add velocity * delta to position yourself.
  • Call look_at on the collision shape.

move_and_slide() uses the body's velocity, slides on walls, and updates is_on_floor(). Gravity comes from get_gravity(), which already includes the project default of 9.8 along -Y.

Where do you enable Use Collision on the arena?

  • On every CSGBox3D and CSGCylinder3D.
  • Only on the CSGCombiner3D root (Arena).
  • On the WorldEnvironment.
  • On the player's capsule, set to layer world.

Collision is generated for the merged solid, and only the root of a CSG tree honors use_collision. Children stay on their boolean operation. The player's mask then includes layer world so move_and_slide() stands on that solid.
