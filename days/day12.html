Concepts you need first

GridMap is a mesh stamp, not a node stamp

A GridMap repeats items from a MeshLibrary on a 3D grid, the way a tile map repeats tiles. Each item is one MeshInstance3D, optional collision on a StaticBody3D child, and an optional NavigationRegion3D child. Other node types on the item are dropped at export. You paint meshes. You do not paint enemies, doors, or the player. Those stay real scenes, placed on top of the grid tomorrow.

Docs constraint

The library scene root is a Node3D. Each item is a MeshInstance3D. The material must sit on the mesh resource (BoxMesh's Material), not on the MeshInstance3D Material Override. Up to one StaticBody3D and one NavigationRegion3D are kept. See Using GridMaps.

Yaw on the body, pitch on the head

A first-person pawn is the same idea as a camera boom in other engines. The CharacterBody3D yaws (turns left and right) so movement and the collision capsule face the look direction. A child Node3D named Head pitches (looks up and down) so the capsule does not tip over. The Camera3D, the spotlight, and tomorrow's ray all hang under Head. Godot cameras look down their local -Z axis.

Input.get_vector takes four actions: negative X, positive X, negative Y, positive Y. Passing move_up as the negative-Y action means W produces y = -1. A Node3D's forward axis is also -Z, so that -1 lands on forward when you multiply the body's basis by Vector3(wish.x, 0, wish.y). No extra flip.

Two fogs

Environment fog tints distant pixels. Volumetric fog is a froxel volume that the torch actually lights. Volumetric fog runs only on the Forward+ renderer. The project you create today must be Forward+, not Mobile or Compatibility.

Re-export after every library edit

The .tres is a copy. Moving a mesh, rebaking navigation, or recoloring a material does nothing to grids that already point at the old resource until you export again and overwrite mesh_library.tres.

Common pitfalls

  • Material Override on the MeshInstance3D. The MeshLibrary export keeps the BoxMesh material slot. A pretty color set on the node is discarded, and the grid looks grey or unshaded.
  • Exported once, edited after. Grids keep the old .tres until you choose Scene > Export As... > MeshLibrary... again and overwrite the file.
  • Center Y left on. The wall mesh is authored from the bottom of the cell. With Center Y on, half the wall sinks under the floor and you can walk over the tops.
  • Painted on floor 1. Ctrl+wheel changes the GridMap floor. Walls in the sky are that mistake. Erase them with the floor field set to the height where they actually are.
  • Pitch written on the body. rotate_x on the CharacterBody3D tips the capsule. Only rotate_y belongs on the body. Pitch is Head.rotation.x.
  • Pause node uses the default process mode. _unhandled_input stops while the tree is paused, so Escape can pause and never unpause. Process Mode Always is required on PauseMenu.
  • Volumetric fog on the wrong renderer. Mobile and Compatibility ignore it. The distance fog can still show. The torch shaft in the air is the volumetric part.
  • Shadow plus a moving light. Temporal reprojection on volumetric fog leaves trails behind lights that move quickly. The headlamp is parented to the camera, so it moves every frame. If the trail bothers you, select SpotLight3D and set Light > Volumetric Fog Energy to 0.4. The docs recommend lowering that value on short-lived or fast lights.

Challenge (optional, about 25 min)

Two wall sconces

Add two OmniLight3D nodes under Main, named SconceA and SconceB. Place them at world (5, 1.4, 3) and (15, 1.4, 11). Warm color, energy 2, range 5, shadows off. Attach one script to both (or duplicate the node) that writes light_energy from a sine wave. Give SconceB a phase offset of PI so they do not pulse together.

Hint: @export var phase: float = 0.0 and sin(Time.get_ticks_msec() / 1000.0 * 3.0 + phase). Do not parent these lights to the player. They are part of the room. Re-read the volumetric-fog note about moving lights before you animate their positions. Energy flicker is enough.

Check yourself

Where must a MeshLibrary item's material live so the GridMap export keeps it?

  • On the MeshInstance3D, in Material Override
  • On the mesh resource (the BoxMesh Material slot)
  • On the StaticBody3D
  • On the GridMap node after painting

Using GridMaps is explicit: the material belongs in the mesh's material slot. The MeshInstance3D material slots are not the ones the exporter reads.

Which menu exports the library?

  • Project > Export
  • File > Save As and pick the tres extension
  • Scene > Export As... > MeshLibrary...
  • The GridMap panel's Tools dropdown

The docs name that menu path. Saving the scene writes a .tscn. The MeshLibrary resource is a separate export.

Why does pitch go on Head instead of on the CharacterBody3D?

  • Rotating the body on X tips the capsule and the floor test
  • Camera3D cannot be a child of a Node3D
  • move_and_slide ignores rotation
  • SpotLight3D only works on a node with no rotation

Yaw stays on the body so the collider faces the look direction. Pitch is a local X rotation on Head, clamped to about plus or minus 1.15 radians.

W is bound to move_up, and the call is get_vector("move_left", "move_right", "move_up", "move_down"). What is wish.y while W is held?

  • 1, because W means forward
  • 0, until you normalize
  • -1, because move_up was passed as the negative-Y action
  • It depends on the camera fov

The third argument is negative Y. Negative Z is also "forward" on a Node3D, which is why the basis multiply does not need another sign flip.

Volumetric fog is enabled, but the air around the torch stays clear. What is the first thing to check?

  • The AudioStreamPlayer bus
  • Whether Center Y is unchecked
  • The pause process mode
  • That the project renderer is Forward+

The volumetric fog page states the effect is Forward+ only. Mobile and Compatibility skip it. Distance fog is a separate Environment toggle and can still be on.
